# Synthetic human pose benchmark: dataset selection

## Study question

Compare the frozen, pretrained outputs of the six existing pose systems—MediaPipe Pose Heavy, MoveNet Thunder, RTMPose-M, YOLO11x-Pose, Sapiens 1B COCO, and OpenPose COCO18—on synthetic RGB human images made with different generation pipelines. This is a zero-shot evaluation: do not fine-tune any model on the candidate datasets. Existing COCO, SyRIP, and COCO-UniHuman outputs remain historical real-image/reference-domain results and are not counted as synthetic data.

The sources below all exceed 300 images or labeled frames by a wide margin. They are candidates pending acquisition, license/access verification, annotation inspection, deduplication, and a small conversion pilot. A website describing a dataset or a successful import is not evidence that its images have been downloaded or its labels are compatible.

## Recommended first cohort

Use four sources for the first study: **SURREAL, LTSH synthetic-only, AGORA, and CURDIE3**. Together they span 2017–2026 and contrast motion-capture/parametric renders, composited multi-person synthesis, scan-grounded scene rendering, and a current Unreal dataset with direct COCO-17 labels. CURDIE3 can anchor the full-17-point comparison after its pilot audit; use its dataset-card caveats when interpreting occlusion and physical realism. Include BEDLAM if clothing simulation is a key research factor. Include JTA if its access key and GTA V conditions are acceptable; it adds a game-engine urban-pedestrian domain but requires a 22-to-17 label audit.

### Hugging Face candidates reviewed

- **CURDIE3** is the best HF match found for the full study: the card reports 1.4M synthetic images, explicit COCO-17 image-space labels, 100 actors, 1,400 animations, three environments, and Apache-2.0. The HF file list shows a 919 GB repository split into 42.9 GB volumes plus a final 16.7 GB volume; because each first volume exceeds the 30 GB/day traffic allowance and labels are bundled with images, do not start a full download for this workstation. Revisit if the authors publish a smaller independently downloadable sample. The card's documented mesh intersections and nonphysical foot contacts make a label/image-quality audit essential. Its paper metadata is new and incomplete; confirm citation and exact license files before publication. [Dataset card and schema](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kikitora/curdie), [file sizes](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kikitora/curdie/tree/main).
- **SynplerHuman** is a secondary HF candidate: the page exposes about 17.2K rows and describes 2D keypoints with visibility and SMPL-X labels, but lists a 242 GB repository and CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Its card's paper/citation fields are incomplete and the `gtkps` schema should be inspected before claiming a COCO-17 mapping. It is less practical than CURDIE3 for this local project. [Dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/princeton-vl/SynplerHuman).
- The Simuletic HF CCTV fall sample has only 111 images, below the preferred 300-image threshold; its card describes a separate 1,000-image full product with a paid license. Its fallen-person-heavy scenarios are useful only as a niche stress test, not a broad general-human benchmark. [Free sample and full-set terms](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Simuletic/CCTV_Incident_Dataset_Fall_Lying_Down_Detection).

The Simuletic free sample is used only as a small pipeline pilot when the larger source datasets are unavailable locally. The 111 PNGs plus YOLO labels total about 172 MB, are labeled CC BY 4.0, and the dataset card says each person has 17 COCO-order keypoints. Its images are strongly fall-dominated and cannot substitute for the planned 300+ image general-human cohorts. [Dataset card, counts, labels, and license](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Simuletic/CCTV_Incident_Dataset_Fall_Lying_Down_Detection).

Start with 500 unique images per source (2,000 total) if the source and storage permit. This is a screening cohort, not a statistical power claim. If access, storage, and thermal headroom are confirmed, expand to 1,000 per source. Sample video datasets at a fixed temporal interval and across source sequences, actions, person scales, visibility/occlusion, camera views, and scene conditions. Never randomly split adjacent frames across subsets. Freeze the sampled file list and SHA-256 manifest before inference. Do not mix train/validation/test source splits; use the designated test split where provided.

## COCO keypoint contract and fairness

1. Convert all verified keypoints into the 17 COCO index slots, but preserve source joint names and conversion provenance in the manifest. Use COCO visibility `v=0` only for genuinely unsupported/unlabeled points, `v=1` for labeled but occluded points, and `v=2` only when labeled and visible under the source's visibility evidence. Never synthesize eyes, ears, nose, ankles, or other landmarks from a nearby but semantically different joint.
2. Keep the current official `pycocotools.COCOeval(iouType='keypoints')` evaluator and its default parameters for full COCO-17 datasets. For sources with incomplete landmarks, report the exact labeled-keypoint coverage and a clearly named shared-joint evaluation; do not present partial-joint AP as full COCO-17 AP. If the shared set requires a different category/sigma vector, record the exact index selection and use the corresponding COCO sigma entries without changing OKS definitions silently.
3. The six adapters have a single-pose contract in the current benchmark. For multi-person sources, define one deterministic target-person crop per sample (or make a new, explicit multi-person protocol for every model). Retain a fixed context margin and test the crop transform against source coordinates. Do not let person selection vary by model.
4. Keep all model weights frozen; use the same input file list and the established timing/precision policy. Report per-source accuracy, joint coverage, model failures, latency, and device. Any crop or label-conversion decisions are fixed before evaluating model outputs.
5. Deduplicate exact RGB files by SHA-256, then check source identity/sequence overlap. Repeated video frames are not independent images. Preserve the source license and do not redistribute raw images or weights unless explicitly allowed.

## Acquisition gates and next execution steps

- First inspect the active project cache and any local source folders for existing licensed archives; validate archive manifests, image counts, label schema, and hashes before downloading duplicates.
- Obtain/confirm registration and license requirements for each source. JTA requires a download key and its stated GTA V/research-use conditions. AGORA, LTSH, and BEDLAM require account registration; BEDLAM explicitly disallows redistribution. Do not accept a dataset agreement or send personal details on the user's behalf.
- For each acquired source, create a small 20-image conversion/visualization pilot. Verify landmarks visually, image-coordinate bounds, visibility labels, person crop transforms, sequence split, and the 17-point serialization before full conversion.
- Only after that audit, implement the loader and immutable handoff manifest, run all six models under the established thermal cutoff, then generate a per-dataset COCOeval report and comparison report. Keep this candidate list separate from active `config/datasets.yaml` until conversion and annotation coverage are verified.
